## The project

- **Question.** Which published numerical periodic orbits of the three-body problem provably exist, and which of
  the "stable" ones are provably stable?
- **Hub.** [physics0](https://github.com/Foundation-0/physics0).
- **Sibling projects.** [point-charges](https://github.com/Foundation-0/point-charges) (results certified by computer) and
  [relative-equilibria](https://github.com/Foundation-0/relative-equilibria).
- **Status.** Not started. The lead is to be named.

**Plain explanation.** Three bodies pulling on each other by gravity usually move chaotically, but some starting
positions repeat exactly: periodic orbits. Searches by computer have published hundreds of thousands of them, found
by numerical integration (following the motion in small time steps).

A numerical orbit is strong evidence, not proof. A computer-assisted proof uses interval arithmetic, which keeps
rigorous bounds on every rounding error, to show that a true periodic orbit exists near the numerical one.

## From the scouting report, not yet re-checked

- **Catalogues.**
  - Li and Liao published 135,445 planar orbits in 2021. Data:
    https://numericaltank.sjtu.edu.cn/three-body/three-body.htm, also on GitHub as
    [sjtu-liao/three-body](https://github.com/sjtu-liao/three-body).
  - The three-dimensional list appeared in Sci. China PMA 69:119515 (2026).
  - Hristov, Hristova and Tanikawa list 971 linearly stable orbits, only as candidates for KAM stability
    ([arXiv:2510.22802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22802), Oct 2025). Linear stability means that, in the linear
    approximation, small nudges do not grow exponentially. KAM stability, after Kolmogorov, Arnold and Moser, is a
    stronger, nonlinear kind: most nearby motions stay close to the orbit forever.
  - An earlier paper by Hristov and co-authors found 12,409 free-fall orbits (all three bodies start at rest) and
    criticised the consistency of earlier lists ([arXiv:2308.16159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16159)).
  - A July 2026 paper finds orbits with physics-informed neural networks
    ([arXiv:2607.23501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23501)).
- **Proofs.**
  - A 2024 note says that proofs that these numerical orbits exist "still remain to be provided, with a few
    exceptions" (Ion, SCSS 2024, https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3754/paper08.pdf).
  - Computer-assisted proofs exist for the figure-eight and a few choreographies, orbits in which all three bodies
    follow one another along one curve (Kapela, Zgliczyński, Simó, 2003–2017;
    https://ww2.ii.uj.edu.pl/~kapela/papers/papers.html).
  - No paper certifying any catalogue was found.
- **Method.** An interval Newton–Krawczyk proof on each orbit's symmetric half-period shooting map (explained in
  [NOTE.md](../NOTE.md)). Tools: the CAPD library, or TaylorIntegration.jl with IntervalArithmetic.jl.
- **Risks.**
  - Long, very unstable orbits blow up interval enclosures, so multiple shooting (splitting the orbit into short
    pieces solved together) is needed.
  - Some initial conditions may be printed with too few digits.
- **Kill criterion** (when to stop): fewer than half of the 50 shortest orbits certify within two weeks.
